# End-to-end tree-building protocol

## Objective

Expand the user's paternal-side genealogy across all branches: paternal and maternal ancestors of each known paternal ancestor, women and maiden names, spouses, siblings and collateral branches when useful. The tree is not limited to the direct paternal surname line. The user's possible Balkan background is an open research question; Bulgaria, Greece and Turkey are equal starting regions, not conclusions.

## Phase 0 — Establish the anchor

Create a case file with:
- the user-designated starting person(s) and exact relationship to the user;
- known names exactly as supplied, including original alphabets;
- date/place ranges and whether exact or approximate;
- documents already held, with provenance;
- oral-history statements attributed to the speaker and date;
- privacy classification for each person;
- open questions and what would count as a useful answer.

Do not infer the father's identity, origin, ethnicity or nationality from the user's surname.

## Phase 1 — Work backwards one event at a time

For each generation, seek the event that explicitly connects the child to parent(s), then independently verify the parents' identities and life chronology. Prefer original or contemporary records:
- birth/baptism;
- marriage (often especially useful for parents, maiden names and witnesses);
- death/burial (useful but informant knowledge and age accuracy vary);
- household/population register;
- military, school, court, land/property, employment and migration records.

A death certificate naming parents may be secondary information; assess who supplied it and when. A baptismal entry may name parents but not prove the later adult identity without correlation. Every record must be evaluated in context.

## Phase 2 — Expand all branches

For every resolved couple:
- identify all documented children, not only the direct-line child;
- search daughters under maiden and married surnames;
- search remarriages and name changes;
- track siblings and witnesses to distinguish same-name people;
- record step-parent, adoption, guardianship and informal care possibilities separately;
- include spouses as people with their own ancestry when within scope.

Do not assume a child list is complete unless the register coverage and survival justify that conclusion.

## Phase 3 — Cross-border and historical-jurisdiction search

For each event, determine the sovereign state, district, municipality, parish and language of administration on the event date. Search both the archive of the historical jurisdiction and likely successor-state repositories. When migration is indicated, follow origin and destination records; a border crossing is not automatically a change of ethnicity or citizenship.

Potential migration pathways to test only when supported by dates/places include Ottoman-era movement, post-1878 border changes, Balkan Wars, World War I, population exchange after 1923, refugee settlement, interwar labour migration, World War II and post-war migration. These are research contexts, not assumptions about this family.

## Phase 4 — Resolve identities and conflicts

Keep separate person IDs for same-name candidates. Build a comparison table with:
- full name and every source spelling;
- age/date range;
- parents and spouse;
- children;
- locality/address/household;
- occupation/status;
- religion/community only when explicitly recorded;
- witnesses/neighbours/associates;
- event chronology;
- supporting and contradicting evidence.

Do not merge candidates merely because multiple details match if the details are copied from one source. Do not force a single answer where the evidence supports multiple candidates.

## Phase 5 — Stop rules

Pause a branch and report the blocker when:
- the locality is not uniquely identified;
- the relevant register is missing or inaccessible;
- two plausible candidates cannot be distinguished;
- a key record is illegible or requires specialist palaeography;
- access depends on the user's legal status or family documentation;
- further searching would repeat already exhausted sources without a new hypothesis.

State what has been checked, what has not, and the next realistic route (archive request, local registry, parish, paid researcher, specialist translation, DNA only if user chooses).

## Deliverables

Maintain:
1. a concise current tree with accepted and provisional edges visually/status separated;
2. a person register;
3. a relationship/evidence register;
4. a source register with archive locators and access status;
5. a search log including negative searches;
6. a conflict/uncertainty list;
7. a next-actions queue ranked by expected information gain, feasibility and cost.

Every report should say what is proven, what is probable, what is unresolved, what is contradicted, and why. Include source citations close to each claim. Never pad the tree with guessed ancestors.
